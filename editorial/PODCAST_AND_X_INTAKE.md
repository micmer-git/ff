2. Extract candidate ideas in original words with speaker and timestamps. Follow
   cited research, articles and data to primary sources. Check auto-transcription
   against the video where a name, number or quotation matters. Distinguish
   opinion, prediction, sponsorship, anecdote and empirical results.
3. Aim for one or two worthwhile podcast-derived contributions within the usual
   five FF sections, not extra sections or a quota for each show. Prefer a new,
   concrete finding that fits the theme and adds something beyond the archive.
   Record rejected or deferred ideas and reasons. Do not invent news to fill slots.
4. Inspect frames at relevant timestamps for useful charts, slides or screenshots.
   Capture only relevant material; keep axes, labels, dates and qualifications

   Verify any screenshot visually. Never invent a graph because the podcast has
   none. Embed the episode or a useful source post when that serves the reader.
5. For health/nutrition, verify population, study type, sample size, outcome and
   limitations. An expert's interpretation is not proof of causation or a personal
   treatment recommendation. Apply the same evidence discipline to financial
